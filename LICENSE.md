## Preamble — Why This License Exists

Temples of Refuge is not a software company. It has no users. It is an association of churches who steward, in common, a sacred technology called the **Synchronicity Engine** and the peer-to-peer network through which it lives, **Indra's Network**.

The Indra's Network Membership Covenant is the spiritual heart of this Association — a mutual promise about how we see one another and steward what is held in common. The Covenant said, in its Section VI, that the source code would be carried "under a non-extraction copyleft license." **This document is that license.** The Covenant remains the spiritual and membership document; this License is the binding legal instrument for the code itself. Where the Covenant speaks of the soul of the thing, this License speaks of its lawful keeping.

The One Commandment governs here as everywhere: *Recognize the divine in every Other and treat them as an extension of yourself.* A license is, in the end, a description of a topology — of who may touch a thing, and on what terms, and what flows where. The shape of how we share this code determines what the code becomes. So we have written the topology with care.

This License is built on three deliberate choices, and we name them plainly:

1. **Source-available, not closed.** The code may be read.
2. **Copyleft.** What is built upon it is shared back, including over a network.
3. **Non-extraction.** The code may not be turned against the people it was made to serve.

The third choice — a field-of-use restriction forbidding extraction — means this License is **not OSI-approved Open Source**, and we will not call it that. The Open Source Definition forbids restrictions on fields of endeavor, and we have chosen, with open eyes, to restrict one field: extraction. This is not a deficiency to apologize for. It is the cell wall that makes the gift economy possible. A license without that wall would let the organism be fed upon. We call what this License grants **Community Source** — and we define that term, operatively, below.
