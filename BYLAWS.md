# BYLAWS OF TEMPLES OF REFUGE

### An Association of Churches

*Adopted by the Founding Stewards, [DATE], 2026*
*Principal Office: Ogden, Utah, United States of America*

---

## Preamble

**Recognize the divine in every Other and treat them as an extension of yourself.**

This is the One Commandment of Temples of Refuge. The Covenant of the Association elaborates the practice it calls for. These Bylaws are its legal and structural form, written so that Temples of Refuge can hold property, ordain ministers, and serve as a federation of religious communities under the laws of the United States.

The spirit lives in the Covenant. The architecture lives here. Where they appear to conflict, the spirit prevails and the architecture is amended.

---

## Article I — The Association

**1.1 Name.** The name of this religious body is **Temples of Refuge** (hereafter "the Association").

**1.2 Form.** The Association is a religious organization organized under the laws of the State of Utah and registered as an **association of churches** within the meaning of Sections 170(b)(1)(A)(i) and 509(a)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code. As an association of churches, the Association is mandatorily exempt from federal income tax under Section 501(c)(3) and Section 508(c)(1)(A) and is not required to file Form 1023. The Inner Council may file Form 1023 voluntarily for a determination letter where it determines that doing so serves the Association's interests with donors, banks, foreign jurisdictions, or other counterparties.

**1.3 Office.** The principal office is 1115 Rancho Boulevard, Ogden, Utah 84404, or such other location as the Inner Council designates.

**1.4 Purpose.** The Association is organized and operated exclusively for religious, charitable, and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3). In furtherance of this purpose, the Association federates a network of constituent **Temples** — religious communities that gather for worship, ordain ministers, steward sacred lands, develop sacred technology, make charitable grants, and provide religious education, each in alignment with the One Commandment and the Covenant. The first member Temple is **Templo da Água Lila** in the Montanhas Mágicas of central Portugal.

**1.5 Prohibited.** No part of the net earnings of the Association shall inure to any private person, except as reasonable compensation for services. No substantial part of the Association's activities shall be lobbying. The Association shall not intervene in any political campaign. The Association shall not discriminate on grounds prohibited by law.

## Article II — Members and Stewards

**2.1 Two-Layer Membership.** Affiliation with the Association exists in two layers, neither of which conveys ownership in the assets of the Association:

(a) **Association Membership** is established by any person making a donation to the Association in an amount they can genuinely offer and signing the Covenant. Association Membership is lifetime and non-revocable except for material breach of the Covenant, fraud, or harm to others.

(b) **Temple Participation** is the relationship between a Member and one or more constituent Temples. A Member may participate in zero, one, or many Temples. Each Temple maintains its own roster of participants under its own bylaws and local covenant, consistent with the Association's standards.

A Member of the Association need not be a participant of any Temple; a participant of a Temple must be a Member of the Association. The Synchronicity Engine maintains the federated record of both layers.

**2.2 Members.** Members of the Association have the right to:

1. Participate in network-wide gatherings, worship, and religious activities;
2. Access the Synchronicity Engine and seek participation in any Temple, subject to that Temple's local practices;
3. Post Intentions through the Engine and earn Tokens of Gratitude through fulfilled acts of service, which confer governance weight upon recognition as a Steward;
4. Receive an annual financial summary from the Association;
5. Petition the Inner Council through the Engine or in writing on matters affecting the Association.

Membership is spiritual affiliation, not proof of personhood, and carries no governance weight on its own.

**2.3 Stewards.** A Member becomes a Steward through the Initiatory Practice — sponsorship by two existing Stewards, in-person recognition at a gathering hosted by any Temple of the Association, a written statement of Stewardship, and affirmation by the Council of Stewards. Stewards are the verified humans of the Association. Recognition through any one Temple confers Stewardship across the network. Stewards carry governance weight **in the Council of Stewards** proportional to their time-weighted Tokens of Gratitude, recognized regardless of which Temple issued them. Tokens of Gratitude do not, of themselves, confer governance weight within any Temple's internal affairs; each Temple governs its own internal life under its own bylaws.

**2.4 Initiatory Practice.** The Inner Council shall maintain a written Initiatory Practice describing the Process in detail. The Practice shall include: a path through service for those without sponsors; the right of every candidate to be heard before the full Council of Stewards; no test of conformity beyond sincere alignment with the One Commandment; periodic review of admission patterns by the Council of Stewards. Member Temples may add local elements consistent with the Practice but may not impose stricter or looser standards than the Practice provides.

**2.5 Departure.** Any Member or Steward may withdraw at any time. The departing person retains the right to export their identity and gratitude history in a portable format. Departure is honored, not punished. No social shunning. Withdrawal from a Temple does not terminate Association Membership or Stewardship.

**2.6 Removal.** Membership or Stewardship may be revoked, only for the causes named in 2.1(a), by two-thirds vote of the Council of Stewards after notice through the Engine or in writing and an opportunity to be heard. Good-faith dissent is not cause for removal. Restorative process precedes punitive action where possible.

**2.7 No Property Right.** Neither Association Membership nor Stewardship confers any ownership, equity, or claim on the assets of the Association. Tokens of Gratitude are religious acknowledgments and governance weight, not securities or distributions of net earnings.

## Article III — The Synchronicity Engine

**3.1 The Operating Layer.** The Synchronicity Engine is the medium through which the Association thinks and decides across its constituent Temples. Intentions are surfaced; attention is gathered; gratitude is recorded; the Council of Stewards expresses its will through the Engine.

**3.2 Three Layers of Decision.**

(a) **Operational decisions** — work, gatherings, projects, grants within budgeted authority — are resolved through the Engine. The Inner Council follows Engine outputs presumptively.

(b) **Fiduciary decisions** — real property, material contracts, employment, litigation, conditional gifts, the annual budget, tax-exempt status — are reserved to the Inner Council, made visibly with rationale published to Stewards.

(c) **Constitutional decisions** — amendments to these Bylaws or the Covenant, recognition of new Temples into the Association, removal of Stewards or directors, dissolution — are made by the Council of Stewards through the Engine, by liquid democracy.

**3.3 Sybil Resistance.** Only Stewards carry governance weight. Members may post Intentions and earn gratitude through fulfilled service; gratitude confers governance weight upon recognition as a Steward.

**3.4 Federated Records.** The Engine maintains the federated registry of Members and Stewards across all member Temples, the cross-Temple recognition of Tokens of Gratitude, and the stewardship records of all Association assets.

**3.5 Engine Policies.** The Inner Council shall maintain written policies governing the Engine, including the Tokens of Gratitude Policy and the rules of liquid democracy. The policies shall be visible to all Stewards, plainly written, and periodically reviewed.

**3.6 Transition.** Until the Engine is functionally complete, the Inner Council shall act in good faith using available tools and migrate decisions to the Engine as it matures.

---

## Article IV — The Council of Stewards

**4.1 Composition.** All recognized Stewards in good standing across all member Temples.

**4.2 Voting by Liquid Democracy.** Each Steward votes with weight proportional to their time-weighted Tokens of Gratitude. A Steward may vote directly or delegate to another Steward, generally or by topic. Delegations are transitive, revocable at any time, and have no automatic expiration. Delegation state is visible to all Stewards.

**4.3 Convening.** The Council convenes continuously through the Engine and at least annually in person at the **Annual Gathering**, which may be hosted by any member Temple in rotation. Constitutional matters are open for a voting window through the Engine appropriate to the matter before any in-person vote.

**4.4 Reserved Decisions.** The matters listed in Section 3.2(c), plus removal of directors of the Inner Council, are reserved to the Council of Stewards. (Selection of each Temple's three representatives to the Inner Council is the responsibility of the Temple itself under Section 5.4.) Quorum and threshold are set by Inner Council policy.

## Article V — The Inner Council

**5.1 Powers.** The Inner Council is the Board of Directors of the Association for all legal purposes. It exercises fiduciary authority over the matters reserved to it under Section 3.2(b) and serves as the Association's collective executive at the network level. The Inner Council does not govern the internal affairs of any individual Temple, which retains sovereignty over its local practice under Article IX.

**5.2 Composition.** The Inner Council consists of **three representatives from each member Temple** of the Association, selected by each Temple under its own bylaws. Among the three representatives of each Temple there shall be **at least one man and at least one woman**; the third may be of any gender. Federated representation across independent Temples is the structural protection against private benefit and concentrated control.

**5.3 Initial Directors.** The initial directors of the Inner Council are the three representatives of **Templo da Água Lila** — Truman Everett Ellis, [NAME OF SPOUSE], and [NAME OF THIRD FOUNDING STEWARD], all Founding Stewards. As additional Temples are recognized under Article IX, their representatives join the Inner Council automatically. "Founding Steward" is a historical descriptor and confers no continuing privilege.

**5.4 Selection by Temples.** Each Temple selects its three representatives under such procedures as the Temple's own bylaws provide, consistent with the principles of these Bylaws. A representative serves until the Temple selects a different representative, the representative resigns, or the representative is removed under Section 5.7. There are no fixed terms; continuous accountability — through each Temple's own processes and through removal by the Council of Stewards — is the discipline.

**5.5 Vacancies.** A vacancy in a Temple's representation is filled by that Temple under its own bylaws.

**5.6 Meetings.** The Inner Council meets at least quarterly and at such other times as any director may call on advance notice through the Engine. Meetings may be held by remote conference. A majority constitutes a quorum. Each director has one vote.

**5.7 Removal.** A Temple's representative may be removed by the Temple at any time. Any director may also be removed, with or without cause, by two-thirds gratitude-weighted vote of the Council of Stewards, or by two-thirds vote of the remaining directors of the Inner Council for material breach of these Bylaws or fiduciary duty.

**5.8 Standard of Conduct.** Each director shall act in good faith, with the care of an ordinarily prudent person in a like position, and in a manner consistent with the One Commandment and the founding principles.

**5.9 Compensation.** Those doing the work of the Association — including directors and Stewards holding Stewardships — may be compensated for that work. Compensation is set by the Council of Stewards through liquid democracy, in accordance with Article VII. Every Steward, including the recipient, may participate; transparency through the Engine is the accountability.

## Article VI — Stewardships and Officers

**6.1 No Single Head.** The Association has no executive head. Operational work is held by Stewards in defined **Stewardships** of specific domains. The Inner Council collectively is the executive at the network level.

**6.2 The Stewardships.** The initial Stewardships of the Association are:

- **Engine Stewardship** — the development, integrity, and continuing care of the Synchronicity Engine and related sacred technology of the Association.
- **Land Stewardship** — the regenerative care, operational support, and physical hosting of land-based and pop-up Temples.
- **Vessel Stewardship** — the holding and pouring of the Association's material resources, the on-chain ledger, the financial affairs, and compliance with applicable tax and reporting obligations.
- **Hearth Stewardship** — the convening of gatherings, the practice of the Initiatory Process, ordination, ritual life, and the care of those in pastoral need.
- **Chronicle Stewardship** — the records, minutes, rosters, federated registry of Members and Stewards across Temples, and the institutional memory of the Association.

The Inner Council may add, combine, divide, or rename Stewardships as the work of the Association evolves.

**6.3 Appointment.** Stewardships are filled by the Inner Council after consulting the Council of Stewards through the Engine. A Steward holding a Stewardship serves until resignation, replacement by the Inner Council after consultation with the Council of Stewards, or removal by two-thirds gratitude-weighted vote of the Council of Stewards. No Steward holds more than one Stewardship at a time. There are no fixed terms; continuous accountability through liquid democracy is the discipline.

**6.4 Officers.** For legal purposes, the officers of the Association are:

(a) **Presiding Steward** — a director of the Inner Council, elected by the Council to preside at meetings and serve as the principal representative of the Association. The Inner Council may rotate the role among directors at any time.

(b) **Chronicle Steward** — the Steward holding the Chronicle Stewardship.

(c) **Vessel Steward** — the Steward holding the Vessel Stewardship.

**6.5 Authorized Signatories.** The Inner Council designates by resolution who may sign on behalf of the Association. No single individual has unilateral authority over disbursements above a threshold set by the Inner Council.

**6.6 Initial Engine Stewardship.** Truman Everett Ellis serves as the initial Engine Steward, subject to the same accountability and compensation rules as any other Steward holding a Stewardship.

## Article VII — Conflict of Interest, Compensation, Indemnification

**7.1 Conflict of Interest Policy.** The Association shall maintain a written Conflict of Interest Policy substantially in the form of the sample policy in Appendix A of IRS Form 1023 Instructions. Each director, Steward holding a Stewardship, and committee member with governing authority shall annually acknowledge the Policy through encrypted signature in the Synchronicity Engine.

**7.2 Compensation.** All compensation paid by the Association shall be reasonable in light of the work performed. Compensation for any director, Steward holding a Stewardship, or other Disqualified Person under Section 4958 of the Internal Revenue Code shall be: (a) set by the Council of Stewards through liquid democracy, in which every Steward may participate including the recipient; (b) informed by comparability data appropriate to the role and the size of the Association; (c) made transparent through the Engine, with the votes and delegations of every participant — including the recipient — visible to all Stewards; (d) documented contemporaneously, including the vote, the rationale, and the comparability data considered. The Inner Council shall maintain a written compensation procedure consistent with this Section.

**7.3 Indemnification.** To the fullest extent permitted by law, the Association shall indemnify each director, Steward holding a Stewardship, and agent against expenses, judgments, fines, and settlements reasonably incurred in connection with any proceeding to which they were made a party by reason of their service, except where adjudged liable for negligence or willful misconduct. The Association may purchase directors-and-officers insurance.

## Article VIII — Finances and Transparency

**8.1 Fiscal Year.** The calendar year.

**8.2 Books and Records.** The Association shall keep complete books and records of account; minutes of all meetings; rosters of Members and Stewards; the federated registry of member Temples; and other records required by law.

**8.3 Sufficiency, Not Accumulation.** The Association shall not accumulate surplus beyond what is reasonably necessary for operations, a prudent operating reserve set by the Inner Council, and responsible care of network-level lands and technology. Surplus beyond the reserve is deployed through the **Community Intention Program**: Stewards stake Tokens of Gratitude on Intentions posted through the Engine; the Inner Council deploys surplus to fulfill the Intentions carrying the greatest community gratitude, in order of priority and within the means available, subject to verification that each fulfilled Intention serves the Association's exempt purpose. Eligible Intentions include grants to member Temples, grants to other 501(c)(3) organizations, mission-aligned charitable projects, need-based hardship support for individuals meeting objective need criteria, infrastructure investment, acquisition of sacred lands, and religious education. Tokens staked on an Intention signal community priority for that Intention; they do not entitle the staker or any individual to a personal distribution. The Association is not a cooperative.

**8.4 Steward Census.** The Inner Council shall maintain through the Synchronicity Engine a continuously updated census visible to all Stewards, including: counts of Members and Stewards across the network and by Temple; the gratitude-weighted standing of the Council; the on-chain ledger of expenditures; the treasury state; lands under stewardship; and the **stewardship record** of each Association asset — the Steward then assigned to it and the chain of stewardship to date.

**8.5 Member Summary.** Annually, the Vessel Steward shall prepare an Annual Financial Summary for Members, including revenues, expenses, assets, liabilities, principal activities, and aggregate compensation.

**8.6 Public Disclosure.** Only as required by law (Form 990 if applicable, Utah annual report, and any voluntary 1023 application).

**8.7 Independent Review.** The Inner Council shall engage independent review or audit of the Association's financial statements as the Council deems prudent and as required by applicable law.

## Article IX — Member Temples

**9.1 Sovereignty.** Each member Temple is a religious community in its own right, sovereign over its local practice, governance, and affairs. The Association does not direct the internal life of any Temple. The Association federates Temples that share alignment with the One Commandment and the Covenant; it does not subordinate them.

**9.2 Becoming a Member Temple.** A community becomes a member Temple of the Association by:

(a) Identifying one or more Stewards of the Association who will steward the Temple's relationship to the Association;
(b) Adopting a local covenant consistent with the One Commandment and the Covenant of the Association;
(c) Committing to recognize the Association's Initiatory Practice for the conferral of Stewardship and the cross-Temple recognition of Tokens of Gratitude through the Synchronicity Engine;
(d) Petitioning the Inner Council for recognition; and
(e) Receiving the consent of the Council of Stewards through the Engine by gratitude-weighted majority vote.

A member Temple may take any organizational form consistent with the law of its jurisdiction — a separate church, a religious association, a foreign-equivalent religious entity, or a pop-up gathering — provided it satisfies the foregoing.

**9.3 First Member Temple.** **Templo da Água Lila**, situated in the Montanhas Mágicas of central Portugal, is recognized as the first member Temple of the Association. The land on which Templo da Água Lila operates is presently held in private title by certain Founding Stewards under an arm's-length use agreement with the Temple, structured to avoid private benefit and consistent with Section 4958 excess-benefit rules. The Inner Council shall ensure that any Association activity in Portugal complies with applicable U.S. reporting requirements (FBAR, Form 990 Schedule F if applicable, Forms 8865 or 5471 if applicable).

**9.4 Pop-up Temples.** Stewards may convene pop-up Temples on a temporary or rotating basis under guidelines published by the Inner Council. Pop-up Temples need not seek separate recognition under Section 9.2 unless they wish to become permanent member Temples.

**9.5 Multi-Temple Participation.** A Member or Steward may participate in zero, one, or many Temples. Tokens of Gratitude are recorded across the network through the Synchronicity Engine and confer governance weight in the Council of Stewards regardless of the Temple in which they were earned. Tokens do not, of themselves, confer governance weight within any Temple's internal affairs; each Temple governs its own internal life under its own bylaws.

**9.6 Forking and Departure of Temples.** A member Temple may withdraw from the Association at any time and continue under its own covenant. The Association honors the departure; tangible Association assets situated at the Temple are addressed under charitable trust principles consistent with these Bylaws. A group of Stewards may also fork the technology and traditions of the Association to form a new community under a new covenant, subject to Article XI.

**9.7 Sacred Technology.** The Synchronicity Engine and related technology are stewarded by the Association for the religious purpose of the network. Source code is openly licensed under a non-extraction copyleft license that prohibits monetization of Member attention and data. Association-titled assets remain Association-titled.

## Article X — Amendments and Dissolution

**10.1 Amendment of Bylaws.** These Bylaws may be amended by two-thirds vote of the Inner Council after reasonable notice to all directors and Stewards through the Engine. Amendments to the One Commandment, Articles II, III, IV, V, VI, IX, X, and this provision require, in addition, gratitude-weighted majority consent of the Council of Stewards through the Engine.

**10.2 Inviolable.** Notwithstanding any other provision, the following are inviolable: (a) the One Commandment; (b) no inurement to private persons; (c) no extraction in the use of Association technology; (d) Steward visibility into the state of the Association; (e) no single executive head; (f) the sovereignty of each member Temple over its internal life; (g) the right of any Temple or group of Stewards to fork under their own covenant; (h) the right of those who choose to stay to continue the Association, regardless of how many have left.

**10.3 Living Documents.** These Bylaws and the Covenant are living documents, evolving with the community through the amendment provisions of this Article.

**10.4 Status as Instrument.** Tax-exempt status is an instrument of this Association's religious purpose, not its source or measure. If the conditions of status come into material conflict with the One Commandment, the Association shall continue its religious mission with or without status.

**10.5 Continuance and Winding Up.** The Association continues so long as any Stewards wish to carry it forward. Departures of Members, Stewards, or Temples — individual or collective, however numerous — do not dissolve the Association; those who remain may continue under these Bylaws.

The Association winds up only when the Inner Council, in unanimous good-faith judgment, finds that no reasonable prospect remains of continuing the religious purpose, or when the Association is dissolved by operation of law. Upon winding up, each asset is directed by its assigned Steward to a successor capable of continuing its stewardship — a 501(c)(3) organization, a foreign equivalent qualified to receive charitable assets, a member Temple continuing as an independent church, or a government for public purpose. The Inner Council ratifies each directive for legal eligibility. Where no steward directive exists, the Inner Council designates a successor in this order of preference: first, land trusts or 501(c)(3) organizations dedicated to the perpetual care of the lands then under Association stewardship; second, member Temples or other 501(c)(3) organizations aligned with the religious purpose; third, as a court of competent jurisdiction may determine for charitable purposes; and last, the federal, state, or local government for a public purpose. No assets shall be distributed to any private person.

## Article XI — Forks of the Network

**11.1 Right to Fork.** Any group of Stewards may fork the source code and traditions of the Association to form a new community under its own covenant. A fork is the use of openly licensed source code and shared inheritance; it is not the transfer of Association assets.

**11.2 Conditions on a Fork.** A forking community shall, before holding itself out as a successor or peer of the Association's technology and tradition: (a) maintain the forked source code under the same non-extraction license; (b) honor the portability rights of departing Stewards; (c) publish its own covenant; (d) adopt a distinct name and visual identity; and (e) refrain from claiming continuity with Temples of Refuge as the source of its religious authority.

**11.3 Asset Transfers to Forks.** Association-titled assets remain Association-titled unless expressly transferred by Inner Council action consistent with these Bylaws and charitable trust principles. The Inner Council may grant trade-name licenses, sub-network recognitions, or charitable grants to a fork that itself qualifies as a 501(c)(3) organization or foreign equivalent, on terms protecting the charitable purpose.

## Article XII — General

**12.1 Construction.** These Bylaws shall be interpreted in light of the One Commandment and the Covenant.

**12.2 Severability.** If any provision is held invalid, the remaining provisions remain in force.

**12.3 Covenant.** The Indra's Network Membership Covenant is incorporated by reference as the spiritual companion to these Bylaws. In any apparent conflict, these Bylaws govern as a matter of corporate compliance while the Inner Council considers harmonizing amendments.

**12.4 Governing Law.** Utah and the United States. Internal religious doctrine is determined by the Association under the First Amendment.

**12.5 Independence.** The Association is independent of any other religious or governing body. These Bylaws supersede any contrary representation in prior documents, including those filed in 2024 under the National Noble Ministers Network template.
