## Preamble

**Recognize the divine in every Other and treat them as an extension of yourself.**

This is the One Commandment. The Cosmology is the ground it stands on. This Charter is the small architecture that lets communities who keep it recognise one another, sit at one table, and remain entirely themselves.

The spirit lives in the Commandment. The architecture lives here. Where they appear to conflict, the spirit prevails and the architecture is amended.

---

## Article I — The Network

**1.1 Name.** The network is called **Temples of Refuge**.

**1.2 What it is.** The network is three things and no more: a **shared cosmology**, this **Charter**, and a **Council** in which each member temple has one seat.

**1.3 What it is not.** The network owns nothing. It holds no land, buildings, money, companies, software or marks. It accepts no donations and sells nothing. It employs no one. It confers no legal, tax or religious standing on any temple or person, and no temple may claim standing by reason of belonging to it.

**1.4 Why.** A body that holds the money or the land of its members becomes the thing the members serve. The network is kept empty on purpose, so that it can only ever be a relationship among temples.

---

## Article II — Temples

**2.1 What a temple is.** A temple is a community that holds a place, a practice or a people as sacred, and says so plainly. It names what is sacred to it in its own words: a Temple of Refuge, a Temple of Sacred Play, a Temple of the Waters.

**2.2 Sovereignty.** Each temple is a community in its own right, sovereign over its practice, its governance, its membership, its property and its affairs. The network does not direct the inner life of any temple.

**2.3 Form.** A temple takes whatever form suits the law of the place where it lives: an association, a church, a company with a purpose, a household, or a gathering with no legal form at all. Its legal and tax standing is its own, settled with its own counsel. The network neither supplies that standing nor answers for it.

**2.4 What a temple promises.** A member temple promises:

1. to keep the One Commandment, and to say publicly what it holds sacred;
2. to welcome visitors from other temples as kin, within its own house rules;
3. to keep its own house in order under its own law, and to make no claim on the network or in its name;
4. to send a delegate to the Council, and to hear what the Council says;
5. to tell the Council plainly when it can no longer keep these promises.

**2.5 Gatherings.** Any temple may convene temporary gatherings in the spirit of this Charter. A gathering is not a member temple unless it asks to become one.

---

## Article III — Becoming a member temple

**3.1 The path.** A community becomes a member temple by:

1. making a written declaration of what it holds sacred, where it stands, and who speaks for it;
2. entering a season of conversation with at least [two] member temples, which includes a visit in person where that is possible;
3. adopting this Charter by whatever process the community uses for its weightiest decisions; and
4. being recognised by the Council.

**3.2 No fee.** Recognition is never conditioned on money, on the use of any product or technology, or on membership in any other body.

**3.3 Founding temples.** The first [two] temples to adopt this Charter together are the founding temples. They hold no privilege over later temples.

---

## Article IV — The Council

**4.1 Seats.** Each member temple has one seat and one voice, whatever its size. A temple chooses its delegate by its own process, and may change its delegate at any time by telling the Council.

**4.2 No single head.** The Council has no president, director or executive. It chooses a **convenor** from among the delegates for a term of [one year], who calls meetings and keeps the record, and who may not serve consecutive terms. The convenor holds no authority beyond those tasks.

**4.3 How it decides.** The Council seeks consent. Where consent cannot be found after honest effort, it decides by [two-thirds] of all seats. Decisions under Article IX require more.

**4.4 What it may decide.** The Council may: recognise a new member temple; record a temple's departure; keep and amend this Charter; keep the Cosmology; settle how the name is used; call gatherings of the temples; and speak for the network, within this Charter, when the temples ask it to.

**4.5 What it may never do.** The Council may not: direct the inner life of any temple; hold money or property, or cause the network to do so; take on debt or employ anyone; bind any temple to a contract; make any legal, tax or religious claim on behalf of a temple; or require any temple to use, buy or promote anything.

**4.6 Record.** The Council keeps a plain record of its decisions, open to every member of every member temple.

**4.7 Costs.** The network has no budget. A temple that hosts a gathering bears its own costs. Temples may share costs among themselves directly, by their own agreement, and no such arrangement passes through the network.

---

## Article V — The name

**5.1 Use.** A member temple may describe itself as a member of Temples of Refuge and may style itself "a Temple of ___" in the manner of Article 2.1.

**5.2 Limits.** The name is never used to raise money for the network, to sell anything, or to suggest that the network vouches for a temple's legal or tax position.

**5.3 On leaving.** A temple that leaves stops describing itself as a member. It keeps its own name.

---

## Article VI — Kindred works

**6.1 Separate.** Temples of Earth, the Synchronicity Engine, and any other enterprise founded by members of a temple are separate from the network. None of them has a seat, and the network has no share in any of them.

**6.2 Free to use, free not to.** A temple may use any tool it finds good. No tool is a condition of membership, and no standing in any tool confers a voice in the Council.

---

## Article VII — Leaving, and forks

**7.1 Leaving.** A temple may leave at any time by telling the Council. Nothing is owed in either direction, because nothing was ever held in common.

**7.2 Setting aside.** The Council may set a temple aside only for persistent and serious breach of Article 2.4, after hearing it, by [three-quarters] of all other seats.

**7.3 Forks.** Any temples may take this Charter and the Cosmology and found a different network. They take a different name, and they do not claim to be this one.

**7.4 Continuance.** The network continues for as long as two temples wish to carry it. Departures, however many, do not end it.

---

## Article VIII — Amendment

**8.1 Ordinary amendment.** This Charter is amended by [two-thirds] of all seats, after every temple has had [sixty days] to consider the text.

**8.2 Inviolable.** The following may be changed only by the consent of every member temple: the One Commandment; Article 1.3, that the network owns nothing and confers no standing; Article 2.2, the sovereignty of each temple; Article 4.2, no single head; Article 4.5; Article 7.1, the right to leave; and this Article.

---

## Article IX — Coming into force

**9.1 Adoption.** This Charter comes into force when the first [two] temples adopt it together. Until then it is a draft, the network has no members, and no one holds any office under it.

**9.2 The founder.** The person who first wrote this Charter holds no seat, office or authority by reason of having written it. Whatever voice they have, they have as a member of a temple.

**9.3 A legal form, if ever.** If the temples later decide that the Council needs a legal form in order to meet or to keep its record, they choose it together, in the place where the Council meets, with counsel, and in a form that cannot hold property beyond what that purpose strictly needs. That decision is an amendment under Article 8.2.
