# Proposed Revised Section VI of the Covenant

*This file is a proposed replacement for Section VI ("The Software") of `COVENANT.md`. It is delivered separately for the Stewards to review before integrating. It does not edit `COVENANT.md` directly.*

*The change in spirit: the Covenant has, until now, been the only place licensing lived — yet the Covenant itself says "this is not a software license." That was a category confusion. The licensing now lives in its own binding document, `LICENSE.md` — the **Community Source License**. Section VI shrinks to a warm, plain-language summary that points there. The Covenant stays what it always was: a mutual promise, not a contract.*

## VI. The Software

The Synchronicity Engine is sacred technology, stewarded in common. The source code of the Engine and the related technologies of the Association are carried under their own binding document — the **Community Source License**, kept in `LICENSE.md` alongside this Covenant. That License is the legal instrument for the code; this Section is only its summary in plain words. Where the two speak of the code and seem to differ, the License governs; where the difference touches the spirit, the One Commandment prevails.

In the language of that License, here is what we promise one another about the software.

### Community Source — Open to You, Not to Extraction

The code is **Community Source**. That means it is source-available to the people who have joined the Association: open to inspection, and — for Members — open to study, to modification, to running your own instance, and to sharing with one another. It is held under copyleft, so that what is built upon the gift stays a gift. And it carries one deliberate restriction — **no extraction** — which means it is honestly *not* "open source" in the formal sense, and we do not pretend otherwise. That restriction is not a fence around the code; it is a fence around *you*, so the technology can never be turned against the community it was made to serve.

### Two Doors, Not One

There are two different things, and the Covenant asks you to hold them apart.

The first is the **freedom in the code itself** — to read it, run it, change it, share it with other Members. That freedom is granted by the Community Source License, and once it is truly given, it is not taken back. A copy lawfully in your hands stays yours, with all its copyleft freedoms, even if you one day leave the Association, and even if you are one day removed. A gift, once given, is a gift.

The second is **access to the living network** — the federated Synchronicity Engine in motion, the Network of Temples, proof-of-personhood, the gift economy as a living social fabric. That is a benefit of Membership, and it depends on the trust that Membership creates. If you depart, or if you are removed under Section VII, you keep your copy of the code; what you set down is the live network and the company of the Association.

### No Extraction

No one — no Member, no Steward, no Temple, and not the Association itself — may use the Engine or anything derived from it to harvest, sell, or monetize the data or the attention of Members; to charge for access to the network in a way that betrays the gift economy; or to strip away community stewardship and run the technology as a centralized platform for private control. The License makes this an operative, enforceable term. The Covenant makes it a vow.

This does not forbid money. A Steward may be fairly compensated for their labor. A Temple may gather local dues and tithes. Gifts may circulate. What is forbidden is extraction — value drawn *out of* the community rather than *through* it.

### Forking Is Not Schism

A group of Stewards may always fork the code and the traditions to begin a new community under its own covenant. Departure is the network reproducing. The License sets the conditions, in harmony with the Bylaws: the fork keeps the same non-extraction Community Source License; it honors the portability rights of any Steward who joins it; it publishes its own covenant; it **takes a distinct name and visual identity** — the bits and the patterns are an open inheritance, but the names and marks of Temples of Refuge remain the Association's own; and it does not carry off Association-titled assets without proper transfer.
